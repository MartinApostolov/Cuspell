# Yona Corpus Pilot — Chapter 1, Pass B

_Source: Yona [Cat Girl Evolution], Bedivere the Mad, Chapter 1 "Quite the Mystery"_
_Source URL: https://www.royalroad.com/fiction/129180/yona-cat-girl-evolution/chapter/2525425/1-quite-the-mystery_

This pass covers the market visit through the discovery of Old Man Jenson's body (roughly Royal Road reader lines 92–160 at the time of analysis).

## B01 — named route and self-assigned label

Source meaning: the narrator takes a food-seeking detour along a route that she personally calls by a particular name.

Finding: Path semantics work. Name already supports contextual naming, but ordinary discourse needs a distinction between public Name and a speaker's private naming convention.

Status: PASS-LONG / DISCOURSE.

## B02 — recurring stop for milk

Source meaning: the narrator habitually visits a named person's home and that person reliably keeps milk available for her.

Finding: Habitual from the first pass solves the recurrence shape. Availability/inventory remains useful ordinary lexicon.

Status: PASS-LONG / LEXICON.

## B03 — late-afternoon optimal-time judgment

Source meaning: the narrator evaluates late afternoon as the best time to visit the market because vendors are packing up and are more willing to give scraps.

Finding: evaluation/comparison from the first pass helps, but superlatives need an extremum over an Evaluation function, and time-of-day is world/calendar vocabulary.

Status: PASS-LONG / LEXICON.

## B04 — unusual low population

Source meaning: fewer people than expected are present at the market today.

Finding: Count plus contextual Few/expected baseline can represent this. The phrase compares actual Count with an expectation model rather than an absolute Few threshold.

Status: PASS-LONG / DISCOURSE.

## B05 — communicative animal sound

Source meaning: the narrator produces a cat vocalization which, in her intended communicative system, means "give me food."

Finding: Sign/Meaning handles this well. Meaning can be speaker/community-contextual, and the quoted sound is Sign content.

Status: PASS.

## B06 — nearest person selection

Source meaning: of two fishermen, the one nearest the narrator responds first.

Finding: Nearest plus Unique resolves this mechanically if no distance tie remains.

Status: PASS.

## B07 — proper-name introduction

Source meaning: that fisherman is identified by the proper name Johnny and then remains available for anaphoric reference.

Finding: the new discourse referent plus Name rules from Pass A solve the architecture.

Status: PASS after discourse extension.

## B08 — generosity and frequency

Source meaning: Johnny is described as unusually generous and sometimes gives the narrator a whole fish.

Finding: generous is evaluative/social; sometimes is frequency/aspect rather than simple Some. Habitual plus a frequency qualifier is needed.

Status: LEXICON / DISCOURSE.

## B09 — elliptical yes/no question

Source meaning: Johnny asks whether the narrator is hungry.

Finding: ordinary speech drops copular/subject material that Cuspell need not copy. The semantic Question is simply Hungry(You).

Status: PASS semantically / STYLE-LOSS only.

## B10 — inventory exhausted

Source meaning: the fishermen have no fish left to sell/give today.

Finding: exact representation can use Count(available inventory)=Zero under a time/location context. SoldOut is a useful economic semantic molecule.

Status: PASS-LONG / LEXICON.

## B11 — rate decreasing

Source meaning: the rate at which weak creatures appear has been decreasing recently.

Finding: the language has Frequency but lacks a general Rate Attribute/derivative pattern and compact trend semantics.

Status: GRAMMAR / LEXICON.

Required resolution: typed Rate/ChangeRate measurements and Increasing/Decreasing trend semantics.

## B12 — promise about a future action

Source meaning: Johnny commits to reserving some food for the narrator on a later occasion.

Finding: Promise already exists as a candidate, and Intend/Plan now exist, but Promise needs a normative result: the speaker creates a social commitment toward future EventSpec content.

Status: LEXICON / GRAMMAR refinement.

## B13 — sensory sequence

Source meaning: the narrator smells a hand, licks it, tastes fish residue, then bites it and tastes blood.

Finding: SenseChannel, Sequence, and Information results handle the structure well. The lexicon needs residue/material-identification concepts only for detailed back-translation.

Status: PASS-LONG / LEXICON.

## B14 — uttered rebuke versus continued affection

Source meaning: a person verbally rebukes the narrator while continuing to pet her.

Finding: Concurrent solves the temporal overlap. The discourse relation is concessive: the continued action contrasts with what the words might predict.

Status: PASS core / GRAMMAR for concession.

Required resolution: ordinary discourse connective Although/Despite or a general Contrast relation between propositions/events.

## B15 — temporary forgiveness/evaluation

Source meaning: the narrator decides not to retaliate strongly on this occasion.

Finding: source phrasing is idiomatic and pragmatic. Core intention can be represented; exact humor/register is style-level.

Status: PASS core / STYLE-LOSS.

## B16 — future conditional threat

Source meaning: if Johnny has no fish again on the next relevant visit, the narrator anticipates conflict/retaliation.

Finding: If plus Future/next-event reference can represent the structure, but next-time needs a successor occurrence selector over Habitual Event instances.

Status: GRAMMAR / DISCOURSE.

## B17 — distance from village and social preference

Source meaning: Jenson's house lies farther from the village center than most homes, and most residents prefer that separation because they judge him negatively.

Finding: Distance/Most/Evaluate can represent the content. The metonymic village/community distinction remains a discourse/world issue.

Status: PASS-LONG.

## B18 — repeated claims about curse and predicted deaths

Source meaning: Jenson has recently been repeatedly asserting that a curse associated with the narrator will cause the village population to die.

Finding: Say/Claim content works conceptually, but Cuspell needs a clean Assert/Claim speech act so a speaker can assert P without the outer narrator endorsing P.

Status: LEXICON / GRAMMAR.

## B19 — visual opportunity inference

Source meaning: because Jenson appears absent, the narrator evaluates the approach as an opportunity.

Finding: source inference is defeasible, not logical entailment. Because in Cuspell currently risks conflating causal/reason relations with inference/evidence.

Status: CUSPELL-DEFECT / GRAMMAR.

Required resolution: distinguish Cause, Evidence/ReasonForBelief, and explanatory discourse Because.

## B20 — unusual open door and probabilistic explanation

Source meaning: an open door conflicts with Jenson's normal habit, so the narrator infers that he is probably about to leave or that something unusual happened.

Finding: ordinary reasoning requires epistemic confidence/probability, expectations, and alternatives. Maybe alone is not enough if Cuspell is to preserve strength of belief.

Status: GRAMMAR / DISCOURSE.

## B21 — listening and absence of sound

Source meaning: the narrator listens and detects no nearby sound, including no breathing sound.

Finding: Sense can return negative observation only relative to channel sensitivity/scope. Absence of perceived sound is not automatically proof that no sound exists.

Status: PASS with an important semantic distinction.

Corpus rule: represent NotDetected(Sound) separately from ZeroSound unless measurement capability justifies the stronger claim.

## B22 — question from unexplained evidence

Source meaning: the narrator asks why the door is open given the apparent absence of the owner.

Finding: wh-question support from Pass A handles the open causal variable only if the answer type can be Event/Explanation rather than a simple Entity/Location.

Status: GRAMMAR refinement.

## B23 — waiting to increase confidence

Source meaning: the narrator waits and continues listening to reduce the chance of being surprised.

Finding: goal/purpose and Sense work, but epistemic confidence is again needed to represent why more evidence changes belief.

Status: DISCOURSE / GRAMMAR.

## B24 — evidence updates belief

Source meaning: seeing the owner's shoes causes the narrator to revise her belief: she now thinks he is probably home, then considers intoxication as an explanation.

Finding: this is explicit belief revision from evidence, not world-state causation.

Status: CUSPELL-DEFECT / GRAMMAR.

Required resolution: Evidence/Supports and confidence-bearing Believe.

## B25 — detecting blood

Source meaning: smell produces Information identifying blood and a direction/source from deeper in the house.

Finding: Sense can return Information, and spatial source localization can be represented. Blood is already a biological Kind candidate.

Status: PASS-LONG.

## B26 — increasing odor intensity

Source meaning: as the narrator approaches, blood-smell intensity increases.

Finding: scalar sensory intensity plus Change/More over a path/time interval is expressible; a general Rate/Trend operator would make it compact.

Status: PASS-LONG.

## B27 — corpse and death

Source meaning: Jenson is motionless and dead, with severe visible bodily damage.

Finding: Dead is already distinct from NotLife and Damage is structured. This is a strong PASS for the life/integrity architecture.

Status: PASS.

## B28 — inference from habitual window state

Source meaning: an open/propped window conflicts with the narrator's memory of the owner's normal behavior, so she flags it as anomalous.

Finding: again requires expectation/habitual state plus Evidence/Belief update rather than causal truth.

Status: GRAMMAR / DISCOURSE.

## Second-pass architecture findings

Pass B adds four important needs beyond Pass A:

1. Epistemic evidence is not causation. The language needs a relation such as EvidenceFor/Supports between Information and Proposition.
2. Belief should carry confidence/strength when prose distinguishes maybe/probably/certainly.
3. Claim/assertion must not commit the outer narrator to truth.
4. Rate/trend language is common enough to deserve typed Rate and Increasing/Decreasing semantics.

It also confirms several earlier decisions:

- Sign/Meaning works well for animal vocalization.
- Nearest/Unique selection works in prose.
- Sense must distinguish not-detected from objective zero.
- Dead/Life/Damage distinctions translate cleanly.
- Concurrent captures simultaneous action better than And or Sequence alone.
