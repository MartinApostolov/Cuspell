## General, non-Yona questions exposed by the complete corpus

These are not missing grammar. They are future **history/world-ontology** decisions.

### 1. Telepathy

The corpus strongly confirms telepathic communication as a recurring real phenomenon.

Current Cuspell already has the communication semantics needed for speaker/content/recipient through Say/Tell. What remains is the channel/concept vocabulary.

Yona `«Telepathy»` is now registered as a Yona Skill New Name.

The generic Cuspell concept remains deliberately unclassified until world history answers whether telepathy was known before executable magic.

### 2. Soul

The later novel repeatedly distinguishes:

- body;
- soul;
- soul-bound body;
- copied body;
- specific identity preserved/recreated through a captured soul;
- Skills/Traits affixed to or modifying a soul.

This provides strong pressure for a future objective `Soul` world concept. It does not establish Cuspell's own pre-magic history of souls, so no root or Old Name is invented here.

### 3. Undead / reanimation

Shade, Wraith and Revenant are Yona-specific names, but the broader ideas of:

- dead-body reanimation;
- soul reanimation;
- soul/body rebinding;
- resurrection-like restoration

are general world concepts worth defining in the eventual default ontology.

Their language can already be described compositionally; the missing work is objective world classification and historical naming.

### 4. Curse / blessing / sacred / divine

Religion is not just belief in the corpus: deities, divine intervention, prayer channels, holy institutions, curses and blessings have objective effects.

Cuspell already has historical deity vocabulary and Worship. A generic `Divine` semantic boundary was deliberately deferred before this pass, and the full novel reinforces that it should be solved as world ontology rather than guessed from English wording.

The same applies to objective Curse/Blessing/Sacred categories if Cuspell's world makes those real categories.

### 5. Healing / regeneration / restoration

The novel repeatedly uses:

- wound repair;
- regrowth;
- rapid regeneration;
- rebuilding tissue after destruction;
- restoring a body from stored/captured information.

These meanings remain compositional with Change, Damage, Injured, Part, identity and world-state operations. A compact Heal/Regrow/Restore vocabulary family may eventually be useful, but the corpus did not show an architectural necessity.

## Compression candidates that remain non-blocking

The full novel still does not force primitive/general roots for convenience words such as:

- Only;

- Until;
- Succeed / Fail / Manage;
- Heal / Regrow / Restore.

They may be added later if readability/frequency justifies them. They are not completeness blockers.

## Architecture conclusion

The corpus evidence is now much stronger than it was after Chapter 50.

A single fiction has exercised Cuspell across ordinary dialogue, combat, social interaction, institutional rules, religion, magic theory, identity, counterfactuals, mental communication, body transformation, death/reanimation, progression systems, Dungeons and divine mechanics for more than 168 numbered chapters without exposing a new structural gap after G16.

That is not a proof that Cuspell is universally complete. It is strong evidence that the next major engineering priority should **not** be speculative grammar growth.

The highest-value next work is:

1. decide the remaining historical/world concepts only when needed (Telepathy, Soul, Divine, undead/reanimation, Curse/Blessing/Sacred);
2. build the minimal objective default world package/classifiers;
3. add runtime reference resolution and world execution;
4. then implement and balance RequiredPower.
