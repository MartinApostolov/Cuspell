# Yona Corpus Pilot — Chapter 1, Pass C

_Source: Yona [Cat Girl Evolution], Bedivere the Mad, Chapter 1 "Quite the Mystery"_
_Source URL: https://www.royalroad.com/fiction/129180/yona-cat-girl-evolution/chapter/2525425/1-quite-the-mystery_

This pass covers the System conversation after the body discovery through the end of Chapter 1 (roughly Royal Road reader lines 164–300 at the time of analysis).

## C01 — imperative interruption of System message

Source meaning: the narrator repeatedly tells the System to stop communicating.

Finding: Stop plus the discourse speech context from Pass A now gives a clean structure.

Status: PASS after discourse extension.

## C02 — rhetorical evaluation of a speech act

Source meaning: the narrator evaluates the System's tone/content as rude.

Finding: Evaluate/GoodFor/BadFor architecture handles the subjective judgment; "rude" is a social-style lexical item.

Status: PASS-LONG / LEXICON.

## C03 — ability acquisition through unlocking

Source meaning: the System says that completing the Quest will cause access to additional System functions and new future capabilities.

Finding: Cuspell can represent Cause/If plus Begin(Can(...)) or Begin(Have/Access(...)), but the corpus needs an Access/AvailableTo state relation and a notion of unlocking as BeginAccess.

Status: LEXICON / WORLD.

## C04 — becoming stronger

Source meaning: after unlocking, the narrator will be able to increase relevant power/capability attributes.

Finding: "stronger" is not one universal scalar. It requires either a registered game-stat Attribute or a comparison over a specified capability profile.

Status: WORLD / LEXICON.

## C05 — evolve / become more than current form

Source meaning: the System offers a future transformation/evolution process that changes the narrator's biological/game form.

Finding: Transform and canonical Kind/profile mappings support this in principle. A game-specific Evolution Process should be a world semantic molecule with deterministic source-to-target rules.

Status: PASS architecture / WORLD.

## C06 — why-question about motivation

Source meaning: the narrator asks why she should want to evolve.

Finding: open Explanation questions now have a typed answer structure. The modal part uses Want/Should/Evaluate.

Status: PASS-LONG.

## C07 — incentive reasoning

Source meaning: the System argues that evolution increases future food-acquisition capability.

Finding: Cause plus Can and evaluation works. This is an argument intended to change Want/Intend, not direct causation of desire.

Status: PASS-LONG / EPISTEMIC-PRAGMATIC.

## C08 — enthusiasm as attitude change

Source meaning: the narrator reacts positively when food gain becomes relevant.

Finding: attitude/evaluation can be represented; emotional interjection is mainly style/pragmatics.

Status: PASS core / STYLE-LOSS.

## C09 — Quest completion as precondition

Source meaning: completing the starter task is required before the rest of the System becomes accessible.

Finding: If can express sufficiency, but "required prerequisite" needs a stronger NecessaryFor/Requires relation so the semantics are not merely one-way implication.

Status: GRAMMAR / LEXICON.

Required resolution: prerequisite/necessity relation distinct from ordinary If.

## C10 — how-question seeking a method

Source meaning: the narrator asks for the method by which she can complete the Quest / solve the mystery.

Finding: open interrogative binders must be able to range over EventSpec/Plan/Method structures, not only ordinary values.

Status: PASS after wh-question generalization / LEXICON for Method.

## C11 — mystery as incomplete Information

Source meaning: the System says discovering the method is itself part of the unknown problem.

Finding: Cuspell lacks a formal KnowledgeGap/UnknownQuestion structure, though Question/Information can approximate it.

Status: LEXICON / INFORMATION refinement.

## C12 — refusal because effort is too high

Source meaning: the narrator declines because she evaluates the required work as not worth the expected benefit.

Finding: Evaluate/Prefer can represent this, but cost-benefit reasoning is verbose.

Status: PASS-LONG.

## C13 — finding hidden food

Source meaning: the narrator searches and finds a stored supply of preserved food.

Finding: Find/Reveal plus Container/Have/Location semantics work.

Status: PASS.

## C14 — stockpile due to paranoia

Source meaning: the narrator attributes the existence of the food stockpile to Jenson's paranoid expectations.

Finding: causal attribution here is partly narrator inference. Evidence/Believe from Pass B prevents the narration from being silently upgraded to objective Cause.

Status: PASS after epistemic extension.

## C15 — sunset as temporal landmark

Source meaning: by the end of the eating/search sequence, the sun is setting.

Finding: world/calendar/astronomical time references can anchor narrative reference time; the language does not need a dedicated universal "sunset" primitive.

Status: PASS-LONG / WORLD.

## C16 — promise from System about safety

Source meaning: the System promises that the narrator will not die and claims the Quest will be beneficial.

Finding: Promise/Assert cleanly separate commitment from truth. The content can include Future(Not(EndLife(...))) and evaluation.

Status: PASS after epistemic extension.

## C17 — conditional danger warning

Source meaning: the System says failure to complete the Quest may lead to death.

Finding: PossibleTo/ProbableTo or a future-risk relation can represent this. If the System is asserting objective causal risk, Cause/conditional probability is world data.

Status: PASS-LONG / WORLD.

## C18 — delayed decision

Source meaning: the narrator says she will consider the choice rather than decide immediately.

Finding: Think/Plan/Intend are not identical. A Deliberate/Consider Process would be useful for active decision-making.

Status: LEXICON.

## C19 — exact three-day narrative interval

Source meaning: the narrator spends three days mostly ignoring the System before reconsidering.

Finding: Duration and a registered day Unit handle the exact interval. Narrative reference time can advance by that duration.

Status: PASS.

## C20 — occasional repeated rebukes

Source meaning: during that interval, the narrator sometimes responds negatively when System messages become intrusive.

Finding: Habitual plus a frequency qualifier handles the pattern, but the ordinary lexicon still lacks concise frequency words such as Sometimes/Often.

Status: PASS-LONG / LEXICON.

## C21 — changing resource trend affects motivation

Source meaning: the decreasing creature-appearance rate implies less future fish, which gives the narrator a reason to care about the mystery.

Finding: the Rate/Trend extension plus EvidenceFor and ImportantTo works. This is a good integrated test of three newly added systems.

Status: PASS after extensions.

## C22 — poor rest due to vigilance

Source meaning: sleep quality decreases because the narrator repeatedly monitors the window.

Finding: Sleep exists, but RestQuality/Vigilance/Attention are world/mind Attributes. The causal relation may be inferred but is straightforward.

Status: LEXICON.

## C23 — nearly tired and nearly hungry

Source meaning: the narrator is close to thresholds for Tired and Hungry without clearly crossing them.

Finding: Cuspell needs a general NearThreshold or DistanceToThreshold construction for scalar/graded predicates.

Status: GRAMMAR / LEXICON.

## C24 — reconsidering an earlier refusal

Source meaning: the narrator forms a new weak intention to investigate the mystery.

Finding: Intend works and belief/attitude change can be represented.

Status: PASS.

## C25 — System response changes narrator's intention

Source meaning: the System's enthusiastic response causes a petty reversal in the narrator's immediate willingness.

Finding: source is pragmatic/comedic. Core semantics can represent Say -> Change(Intend/Want), but tone and pettiness are style-level.

Status: PASS core / STYLE-LOSS.

## C26 — waking into an anomalously empty house

Source meaning: after sleeping, the narrator notices that expected people, smell, and sound are absent.

Finding: Expect + NotDetected now gives a much more faithful representation than objective absence predicates. The corpus validates the epistemic separation.

Status: PASS after epistemic extension.

## C27 — repeated anomaly recognition

Source meaning: the narrator notices that she has been calling many recent events strange.

Finding: this is metalinguistic/discourse self-reference: Count/Record over previous evaluations/utterances. Expressible but cumbersome.

Status: PASS-LONG / DISCOURSE.

## C28 — unanswered call

Source meaning: the narrator vocalizes loudly but receives no response.

Finding: Say/Sound plus a bounded waiting interval and NotDetected(response) works.

Status: PASS-LONG.

## C29 — crowd and preparation event

Source meaning: a crowd is gathered near the market/dock while several fishermen prepare a large boat with serious demeanor.

Finding: Group/Collection, spatial relations, Concurrent, and artifact Processes handle the core. "serious expression" is evaluative/body-language lexicon.

Status: PASS-LONG / LEXICON.

## C30 — overheard causal rule about spawn rates

Source meaning: one character reports a rule: low appearance rates for weak creatures indicate that stronger creatures are nearby.

Finding: Rule + Assert + EvidenceFor is an excellent fit. The reported rule need not be treated as narrator truth until separately endorsed.

Status: PASS after epistemic extension.

## C31 — probable deaths

Source meaning: a character says absent fishermen are probably dead.

Finding: Assert(speaker, ProbableTo(speaker, Dead(...))) preserves both source attribution and confidence.

Status: PASS.

## C32 — emotional reaction of listener

Source meaning: a child reacts with distress to the claim.

Finding: emotion lexicon remains sparse but does not expose a grammar failure.

Status: LEXICON.

## C33 — husband missing from expected place

Source meaning: Sally is present but her husband is not observed among the expected people.

Finding: NotDetected and discourse referents again prevent conflating "not seen here" with "does not exist" or "is dead."

Status: PASS.

## C34 — asking whether events relate to the Quest

Source meaning: the narrator asks whether the current crisis is connected to the starter task.

Finding: Question plus Relation/Explanation works. The System's uncertain response is epistemic rather than binary truth.

Status: PASS.

## C35 — motivation becomes personal

Source meaning: the narrator decides to perform the Quest because the missing fisherman threatens her main food source.

Finding: Intend/Plan plus ImportantTo and Cause/Evidence structures represent the decision. The exact comedic motivation is style-level.

Status: PASS-LONG.

## Third-pass architecture findings

The remainder of Chapter 1 adds three useful refinements:

1. **Prerequisite/necessity** should be distinct from ordinary If.
2. **Near-threshold scalar language** is useful for "almost X" and other graded-state expressions.
3. Open questions should range over **Method/Plan/Explanation/EventSpec** structures, not only Entity-like values.

Otherwise the new discourse/epistemic additions substantially reduce the number of core grammar failures in the later part of the chapter.

Chapter 1 therefore already shows measurable convergence: early prose produced many architecture defects; later prose mostly produces lexicon/world-model gaps and style loss.
